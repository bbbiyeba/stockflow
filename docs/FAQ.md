# FAQ

Questions a supply chain or data science reviewer is likely to ask. Numbers come from the latest pipeline run on synthetic data; see `reports/` and `outputs/run_manifest.json`.

## The results

**Is any of this real company data?**
No. Every number comes from a synthetic network of 25 branches and 300 SKUs, generated by `data/generate.py`. It is built to look like ERP and warehouse extracts, including outages, keying errors, returns and stock-outs. The method transfers to real data; the dollar figures do not.

**How do I know the forecast accuracy isn't flattering itself?**
- **Held-out reporting:** accuracy is reported on a 28-day month that played no part in choosing or tuning any model. Model choice uses only the three earlier months.
- **Baseline comparison:** results are compared with seasonal naive, which repeats last week, and with four other simple models.
- **Losses are shown:** where the model loses, by group or series by series, is in `reports/holdout_where_selected_loses.csv` and on the dashboard's Forecast accuracy page.

**Why is daily error about 50%?**
- Many products sell a few units a day or less, so a single day is mostly noise.
- Planning uses lead-time and 28-day totals, where the error is 12.9%.
- Daily WAPE is shown anyway because it is the honest, standard measure.

**Where do the savings come from, and can I trust the dollar amount?**
- **Net savings** = the drop in total cost: freight, plus expected lost sales, plus the holding and obsolescence cost of excess left in place.
- **Cost settings:** the price of a lost sale (0.6 × unit cost) and the holding rates are estimates in `config.yaml`. They need agreeing with finance before the dollar figures are trusted.
- **Formulas:** the dashboard spells out every formula under "Before / after, and how these numbers are calculated".
- **Robustness:** with any one assumption changed, net savings stay between $171k and $235k (`reports/sensitivity.csv`).

**Why does the optimiser leave more positions at risk than the greedy rule (72 vs 56)?**
- It minimises total cost, so it skips fixes that cost more in freight than the lost sales they prevent.
- It still saves more overall: $203k against $193k.
- If the business prefers to protect availability, raising `stockout_cost_multiplier` changes that trade-off.

## Running it

**What does it cost to run?**
- **Base network:** the full pipeline takes about 6 minutes on one 4-core, 15 GB machine, plus 2.5 minutes for the robustness re-solves (measured 380 s and 148 s). The 10× network (250 branches, 3,000 SKUs) completes on the same machine; stage timings are in [AUDIT.md](../AUDIT.md).
- **Daily runs:** that is about 4.5 machine-hours a month. At an assumed $0.20 per machine-hour (check your cloud's price), that is well under $5 a month.
- **Dashboard:** a small always-on container.
- **Licences:** none. Everything is open source, with HiGHS as the solver.

**How does it connect to our systems?**
- **Inputs:** the pipeline reads seven extracts: the branch master, the item master, daily sales, stock movements and on-hand counts, open purchase orders, promotions, and lane costs. Each is defined with validation rules in [DATA_CONTRACT.md](DATA_CONTRACT.md).
- **Running on real data:** point `paths.raw_dir` at the real files and run `python run_pipeline.py --skip-generate`. A bad file stops the run with a readable message.

**Does it execute transfers in the ERP?**
- Deliberately not. It recommends; a planner approves or rejects each transfer and then raises it in the ERP.
- Every decision is appended to an audit log with the reviewer's name, the time and the pipeline run.

**What about data privacy and security?**
- **Data:** the model uses no personal data, only sales by branch and SKU. That data is still commercially sensitive.
- **Controls:**
  - OIDC sign-in, and an approver role for decisions;
  - masking of dollar values for demos;
  - read-only access to the outputs.
- **Repository:** no credentials are kept in it.
- **Production setup:** keep extracts and outputs inside the company network, and use a read-only database account. See [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

**How would it be monitored after go-live?**
- **Weekly check:** `python -m src.monitoring` compares live forecast error and bias with the held-out baseline, checks input data for drift, and signals when retraining is due.
- **Run records:** every run writes a manifest with the config hash, package versions and output hashes, so any number can be traced to the run that produced it.
- **Details:** see [MONITORING.md](MONITORING.md).

## Rolling it out

**What would a pilot look like?**
A 12-week shadow pilot at 2–3 branches:

| Weeks | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Connect data | Connect the seven extracts and validate them |
| 5–10 | Shadow pilot | Planners review recommendations weekly and approve or reject them. Recommendations are compared with what actually happened, and rejections show where cost assumptions are wrong |
| 11–12 | Decide | Decide on wider rollout using measured forecast error, stock-outs avoided and freight spent |

**What is needed from the business?**
- Read-only extracts for the pilot branches.
- One planner willing to review recommendations weekly.
- Time with finance to agree the cost settings.

**What are the known limitations?**
See the README section "Assumptions and limitations", [MODEL_CARD.md](MODEL_CARD.md) and the deferred items in [AUDIT.md](../AUDIT.md). In short:
- results so far are on synthetic data;
- the plan looks one replenishment cycle ahead;
- safety stock uses a normal error model;
- lost sales are excluded rather than reconstructed;
- storage capacity is counted in units, not cubic volume.
